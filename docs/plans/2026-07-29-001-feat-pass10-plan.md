## Revision history

**v1 -> v2.** v1 was titled "resolve the grain trend" and built its only alpha-bearing test on the 33
Stage F masks that complete the k=15 rung. An adversarial review killed it on four counts, all
verified against the repo:

1. **The 33 are the DISCOVERY DRAW.** `p8_cluster_grain.py`'s W1 scan scored every frozen config on
   Stage F's 72 masks, and `p8_prereg.md` chose the cluster-grain hypothesis *from that scan*. So the
   ancestor of everything the k=15 rung tests was discovered on these masks. v1's defence — "never
   scored through the campaign pipeline" — is a pipeline distinction, not an inferential one:
   re-running redraws the seed noise, not the true per-mask outcome the scan selected on. BLOCKERS
   #28 (winner's curse, measured at +0.083 vs -0.044 on a discovery draw) and #31 (the honest draw
   set is per-config) forbid it. **Campaign N's 37 were the only winner's-curse-free 5of9-conditional
   masks that will ever exist on this corpus, and pass 9 spent them.**
2. **The test was unpassable, not merely underpowered.** Measured: k=15 at n=37 gave a ratio CI of
   width 2.155. At n=33, width ~2.3 and lower gap ~0.50, so `CI_lo >= 0.5` needs a point ratio ~1.0 —
   against an adjoining measurement of 0.30 and an attainable maximum near sqrt(r). v1 hand-waved
   where it should have computed.
3. **Pooling to 70 resolves nothing.** Width ~1.57, lower gap ~0.34; the interval still contains
   k=3's and k=5's entirely. Even the best buyable design (the 70 at depth 4) gives ~0.60 and still
   overlaps. v1's falsification row "pooled 70 CI tightens above 0.5" needs ~4x more masks than exist
   in the universe.
4. **v1's escape hatch was incoherent.** It named U3 as "the pass's main hope" for the trend, but U3
   runs on campaign O's k=3/k=5 masks only and never touches k=15 — the rung whose uncertainty is
   binding.

Two further corrections: v1's U2 regime argument was **backwards** (below), and v1 spent its whole
GPU budget on a re-read whose direction was already measured while deferring the one threat with
unknown direction.

## Summary

**The headline is an admission, and it costs no GPU to establish.** Conditional on C5, the
5of9 cluster-mask population is capped at C(8,4) = 70. Campaign N holds the only 37 that are free of
the winner's curse and they are spent. No design available on this corpus tightens the k=15 rung
below a CI width of ~0.6, which still overlaps k=3 and k=5 completely. **The grain trend is
structurally unresolvable here.** Pass 9 reported it as "suggestive and unestablished"; pass 10
upgrades that to "unestablishable", which is a stronger and more useful statement because it tells
the next corpus what to design for.

Given that, pass 10 spends its effort on the threats that *are* resolvable:

1. **U1 — complete the k=15 rung as a CENSUS** (132 retrains, ~90 min). No prereg, no alpha, the 33
   flagged as discovery-set masks expected to read high. Completes the record and puts the full
   conditional population on one pipeline at depth 4.
2. **U2 — the datamodel at sub-cluster grain** (zero GPU), with v1's regime claim corrected.
3. **U3 — a SECOND PARTITION DRAW at k=3/k=5** (1600 retrains, ~18h), replacing v1's depth-4
   re-read. This is pass 9's own named robustness check and the only remaining threat whose direction
   is unknown.
4. **U4 — the bar-standard question**, with a stated discriminator or an honest label as judgment.

## Problem Frame

**Why the trend cannot be resolved, stated once so the pass never re-litigates it.** The estimand is
the ratio at k=15 conditional on C5-in-mask at a fixed 75-demo training set. That conditions the mask
universe to C(8,4) = 70 subsets — a hard combinatorial cap, not a sampling budget. Of those, 37 are
winner's-curse-free (campaign N) and were scored once; 33 are the Stage F discovery draw. Bootstrap
width scales ~1/sqrt(n), so from the measured 2.155 at n=37: n=70 gives ~1.57 at depth 2 and ~0.60 at
depth 4. k=3 and k=5 have intervals of width ~0.37 centred at 0.36. A k=15 interval of width 0.60
cannot be separated from them. **No purchasable design closes this.** The only escapes are a larger
corpus (thread 5, off-box) or a different estimand.

**A conceptual point v1 missed.** Once the 70 are complete, a bootstrap *over masks* is the wrong
uncertainty: there is no mask sampling left, because the conditional population is exhausted. What
remains is seed noise and the conditioning choice. The census must report it that way rather than
quoting a mask-resampling CI over a complete population.

**Why the second partition draw is the right 18 hours.** Pass 9's rungs at k=3 and k=5 rest on a
single committed partition (`p9_grain.GROUP_SEED = 20260728`), so they carry partition-sampling
variance that k=15 structurally cannot — a caveat pass 9 wrote down and a check `p9_prereg.md`
explicitly named as "the cheap robustness check if the curve comes out close". It came out close
(0.356 vs 0.365). Its direction is **unknown** and it could move both rungs. The alternative,
v1's depth-4 re-read, has a **known** direction already measured directly (#42: the same 5of9 masks
give 0.666 at depth 2 and 0.496 at depth 4) and answers a methods question #29 already half-answered
("the mean ratio-to-ceiling is flat across allocations"). The honest `rho/sqrt(r)` scale that v1's R5
wanted is **already computed** in `confirm_oseries.csv`. Spending 18h to confirm a known direction
while an unknown-direction threat sits deferred is the wrong allocation.

## Key Technical Decisions

**KTD1 — No prereg on the 33. The census is descriptive.** #28/#31. The 33 are the discovery draw;
they are expected to read high, and that expectation is stated in advance rather than discovered
afterwards.

**KTD2 — The census is at depth 4, not depth 2.** Campaign N's 37 already have depth 4 on disk. Depth
2 would leave the completed rung off whatever curve the pass ends with, which was an internal
incoherence in v1. 33 masks x 4 seed slots = 132 retrains, ~90 min.

**KTD3 — The one preregistered test is the second partition draw.** Family of two (k=3, k=5), alpha
0.025 one-sided each, on the same absolute-bar rule pass 9 used. The hypothesis of record is *not*
"the bar is cleared" — pass 9 answered that — but **agreement with pass 9's partition**: the
preregistered claim is that each rung's ratio on the new partition falls inside pass 9's CI for that
rung. A failure is the interesting outcome, because it would mean the k=3/k=5 numbers are
partition-draw artifacts.

**KTD4 — v1's U2 regime argument is withdrawn.** As masks/coefficients: demo grain 24/135 = **0.18**
(under-determined — the regime the datamodel earned its reputation in); cluster grain 149/9 = 16.6;
sub-cluster 400/45 = **8.9** at k=3 and 400/27 = **14.8** at k=5. The sub-cluster designs are
*over*-determined, and k=5 is close to the cluster grain pass 9 declined to call like-for-like. They
are the **opposite** regime from 24-vs-135, not "much closer" to it. The error came from HANDOFF
thread 2 and was repeated without checking. The experiment is still free and worth running, but what
it tests is whether the datamodel's advantage survives non-trivial design width — **a win says
nothing about the p >> n regime.**

**KTD5 — U4 needs a discriminator or a label.** v1 cited the permutation nulls as discriminating
between "the bar is mis-specified" and "attribution does not work at 135 demos". Both readings
predict exactly the observed weak-positive nulls, so the evidence discriminates nothing. Either
supply an external anchor (what `rho/sqrt(r)` a method considered working achieves on a published
benchmark) or label U4 a judgment. The genuine discriminator is corpus-size scaling, which is
thread 5 and off-box.

**KTD6 — Depth-4 re-read of campaign O is deferred, not cancelled.** If the partition draw lands
early and the box is free, it is the next candidate. It is a footnote, not a unit.
